# Introduction

This project uses two datasets to practice different data visualization methods. Part 1 focuses on Tampa weather data from 2022, mainly looking at temperature distributions and precipitation patterns. Part 2 uses the concrete strength dataset and focuses on how concrete strength changes with age and mix variables.

# Part 1: Tampa Weather Data



The weather data includes daily temperature and precipitation measurements from Tampa in 2022. I cleaned the missing values, created a date column, and added month labels so the data could be grouped more easily.

## 1A. Monthly Faceted Histograms of Maximum Temperature

<img src="Marsh_project_03_files/figure-html/monthly-histograms-1.png" alt="Faceted histograms showing the distribution of Tampa maximum daily temperatures by month in 2022." style="display: block; margin: auto;" />

The monthly histograms show the seasonal shift in Tampa temperatures. Cooler daily highs appear in the early and late parts of the year, while summer months are more tightly grouped around higher temperatures.

## 1B. Combined Monthly Density Plot

<img src="Marsh_project_03_files/figure-html/combined-density-1.png" alt="Combined density plot showing Tampa maximum daily temperature distributions by month." style="display: block; margin: auto;" />

This combined density chart makes it easier to compare month-to-month temperature changes. The curves move toward hotter temperatures in the middle of the year, which matches the expected seasonal pattern.

## 1C. Faceted Monthly Density Plot

<img src="Marsh_project_03_files/figure-html/faceted-density-1.png" alt="Faceted density plots showing monthly distributions of Tampa maximum daily temperatures in 2022." style="display: block; margin: auto;" />

The faceted density plot shows the same temperature pattern with less visual overlap. This version is easier to read because each month has its own panel.

## 1D. Ridgeline Plot

<img src="Marsh_project_03_files/figure-html/ridgeline-plot-1.png" alt="Ridgeline plot showing the distribution of Tampa maximum daily temperatures by month in 2022." style="display: block; margin: auto;" />

The ridgeline plot makes the seasonal shift clear because the monthly distributions move toward higher temperatures during the summer. The quantile lines help mark the center of each month’s distribution.

## 1E. Precipitation Visualization

<img src="Marsh_project_03_files/figure-html/precipitation-plot-1.png" alt="Bar chart showing total monthly precipitation in Tampa during 2022, with fill showing the number of rainy days per month." style="display: block; margin: auto;" />

The precipitation chart shows that rainfall was uneven across the year. Some months had more total precipitation and more rainy days, while other months were much drier.

The concrete dataset is useful because it connects material strength to age and mix ingredients. I cleaned the column names, renamed the main variables, and created a water-to-cement ratio to use as an additional visual feature.

## 2A. Distributions of Two Continuous Variables

<img src="Marsh_project_03_files/figure-html/concrete-distributions-1.png" alt="Faceted histograms showing the distributions of concrete strength and cement amount." style="display: block; margin: auto;" />

The distribution chart shows that concrete strength and cement amount do not follow the same shape. Strength is spread across a wide range, while cement amount is more concentrated around certain values.

## 2B. Concrete Strength vs Age

<img src="Marsh_project_03_files/figure-html/strength-vs-age-1.png" alt="Scatterplot showing concrete strength compared to age with a smoothed trend line." style="display: block; margin: auto;" />

This chart shows that concrete strength tends to increase with age, especially early on. The pattern is not perfectly linear, which makes sense because concrete strength also depends on the mix ingredients.

## 2C. Scatterplot with Aesthetics
